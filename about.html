Luke Johnson grew up in Tahlequah, Oklahoma, the seat of Cherokee County. He started writing as a teenager, first through school and community writing, then through opinion columns submitted to his hometown newspaper, the Tahlequah Daily Press. Those columns were picked up through regional and national syndication, carrying his byline into newspapers across Oklahoma, Texas, New York, Pennsylvania, Indiana, Illinois, and West Virginia, and onto Yahoo.

Luke is a member of Generation Z, and that vantage point shapes almost everything he writes. Rather than commenting on young people from a distance, he writes from inside the experience of growing up alongside smartphones, social media, remote learning, and a political culture that often talks about his generation without talking to it.

Why he writes

Luke started writing columns because he noticed a gap: adults were writing about education policy, social media, and youth culture, but young people were rarely asked to weigh in on the debates that were about them. Submitting his first columns was his way of putting a direct, first-person perspective into that conversation instead of leaving it to be described secondhand.

That motivation, writing from inside an experience instead of about it from the outside, runs through his columns on education and technology, the personal narrative of Becoming You, the empathy of We're All the Same, and now Dead Air America.

Journalism & opinion

His columns have addressed technology and social media in adolescence, the balance between oversight and trust in education policy, the pressures facing teenagers, the spread of automated surveillance into small towns, and the value of youth civic engagement. In November 2026 the Tulsa World published his op-ed on why an election he can't yet vote in is already about him. The full list is on the writing page.

Luke treats opinion writing as documentation as much as argument. Instead of hot takes, he aims to describe clearly what it is actually like to grow up inside the systems he covers, so readers get a first-person account rather than a secondhand summary.

Civil discourse

A consistent thread runs from We're All the Same through his columns and into Dead Air America: an interest in how people talk to each other across real differences. Luke's commentary favors description over persuasion. He writes to make the mechanics of disagreement, media consumption, and institutional trust visible, so readers can reach their own conclusions with better information.

The books

We're All the Same focuses on autism awareness, empathy, and the idea that every person deserves to be understood and included. Becoming You is a book of advice from a teenager on growth, identity, confidence, and resilience. His forthcoming Dead Air America is a book-length work of political nonfiction and media criticism that looks at modern America as a system and asks how much trust still holds it together. More on the books page.

Beyond the page

Luke has spoken at public events including a state legislative forum on education, where he put questions directly to lawmakers about classroom needs and student experience. He is available for interviews, panels, and speaking engagements on the topics he writes about.

He is also an entrepreneur and web developer, and he builds and maintains his own digital presence, including this site.

Questions

Who is Luke Johnson?

Luke Johnson is an American teenage author, journalist, and political columnist from Tahlequah, Oklahoma. He is the author of We're All the Same and Becoming You, and is completing an upcoming political nonfiction book, Dead Air America.

What are Luke Johnson's political views?

Luke's writing focuses on describing American institutions, media, and civic discourse rather than advocating for a party or ideology. His columns and Dead Air America approach politics as a subject to examine clearly and honestly, aiming to inform readers across the political spectrum.

What topics does Luke Johnson cover?

Political commentary, journalism and media criticism, American culture and institutions, education policy, technology and social media, and the experience of growing up as part of Generation Z.

Where has Luke Johnson's writing been published?

His columns have appeared in the Tulsa World, the Tahlequah Daily Press, the Northeastern Journal, and dozens of newspapers across Oklahoma, Texas, Indiana, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois, and West Virginia through CNHI syndication, as well as on Yahoo.
