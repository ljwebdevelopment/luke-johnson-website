About
Luke H. Johnson

An Oklahoma writer speaking from inside the questions his generation is living.

Luke H. Johnson is a published author, independent columnist, and youth political advocate from Tahlequah, Oklahoma. His columns have been carried by 32 outlets in seven states, including the Tulsa World and the Tahlequah Daily Press, with CNHI syndication across several states, and he is the co-founder and publisher of the Northeastern Journal. He is the author of We're All the Same and Becoming You, and he is finishing Dead Air America, a work of political nonfiction about growing up while a lot of hard things in this country were becoming normal.

Background

I grew up in Tahlequah, Oklahoma, the capital of the Cherokee Nation, and I'm proud to be Cherokee, even though it's a small enough part of my ancestry that I don't lead with it. A lot of what I know about this place I learned from my grandmother, who took my brother and me to nearly every museum and historic site in town when we were young, and who years later became my co-founder and editor.

Why I write

Most of the conversations about young people, whether they're about phones or schools or what my generation is supposedly like, happen without many young people in the room, and I started writing because I wanted at least one first-person account in the mix. At fourteen I spoke at a state legislative forum on education, and the way that room handled a kid's question made me want to put the question somewhere it couldn't be waved off as easily, which turned out to be the opinion page.

Journalism and opinion

My first column, "Politics Isn't Just for Adults," ran in four Oklahoma papers after an editor at the Tahlequah Daily Press invited me to write, and since then I've published 23 columns, carried by 32 outlets in seven states, with some of them picked up by Yahoo. I write about schools, social media, surveillance, and the ways politics reaches people my age, and I try to document what I'm seeing more than hand out hot takes, because I don't think I have all the answers and I'd rather show readers what happened and leave them room to think about it.

My grandmother and I also co-founded the Northeastern Journal, a free, independent news site for northeastern Oklahoma, with her as editor and me as publisher, so that two generations can cover the same corner of the state side by side.

Disagreeing well

I'm not registered with either party, and I try to hear both sides before I make up my mind about anything. My writing leans one direction and I'm upfront about that, but I believe two people can disagree about politics without either of them being a bad person, which is why I've made a point of calling and interviewing people I disagree with instead of only writing about them from a distance.

The books

We're All the Same is a book about autism awareness and inclusion, and Becoming You is a book of advice about growth and identity. My next book, Dead Air America, is political nonfiction and media criticism about growing up while the country changed around me, and I wrote most of it at fifteen, while most of what it describes was still happening.

Questions

Who is Luke H. Johnson?

Luke H. Johnson is a published author, independent columnist, and youth political advocate from Tahlequah, Oklahoma. He is the author of We're All the Same and Becoming You, and is completing an upcoming political nonfiction book, Dead Air America.

Is Luke H. Johnson the British businessman Luke Johnson?

No. Luke H. Johnson is an American teenage author and columnist from Tahlequah, Oklahoma. He is not the British businessman and former Financial Times columnist Luke Johnson, and is not related to him.

What are Luke H. Johnson's political views?

Luke is not registered with either party and tries to hear both sides before making up his mind. He is upfront that his writing leans one direction, and he believes two people can disagree about politics without either of them being a bad person.

What topics does Luke H. Johnson cover?

Political commentary, journalism and media criticism, American culture and institutions, education policy, technology and social media, and the experience of growing up as part of Generation Z.

Where has Luke H. Johnson's writing been published?

His columns have appeared in the Tulsa World, the Tahlequah Daily Press, the Northeastern Journal, and dozens of newspapers across Oklahoma, Texas, Indiana, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois, and West Virginia through CNHI syndication, as well as on Yahoo.
